Why India is at risk

A tsunami is a series of very long ocean waves, usually caused by an undersea earthquake that suddenly lifts or drops the sea floor. Underwater landslides and volcanic eruptions can also cause them.

Two main source zones near India

Andaman-Sumatra subduction zone

Where the Indian plate slides under the Burma plate. Source of the 2004 tsunami. Threatens the east coast and the Andaman and Nicobar Islands.

Makran subduction zone

In the Arabian Sea, off Pakistan and Iran. The 1945 earthquake here caused a tsunami that hit Gujarat and Maharashtra. Threatens the west coast.

Many coastal areas are densely populated and have fishing villages, ports, and tourism, so even a moderate wave can cause large losses.

Case study: 26 December 2004

About 6:29 AM IST
A magnitude 9.1-9.3 earthquake strikes off Sumatra, Indonesia.
Within minutes
Waves reach the Andaman and Nicobar Islands.
Around 2 hours later
Waves hit the coasts of Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Kerala, and Puducherry.
Aftermath
Thousands of lives lost, fishing communities destroyed, and widespread damage to homes and boats.

Lessons learned

  • India had no tsunami warning system in 2004.
  • After 2004, India set up the Indian Tsunami Early Warning Centre at INCOIS (operational from 2007).
  • Coastal vegetation like mangroves can reduce wave energy.
  • Community awareness and evacuation drills save lives.

Preparedness and early warning

INCOIS monitors earthquakes using seismic stations and checks sea level with tide gauges and deep-ocean buoys. Warnings go out by SMS, email, radio, and TV to state authorities and coastal communities.

Common questions

What are the natural warning signs?

Strong or long shaking near the coast, a sudden rise or fall of the sea, and a loud roar from the ocean.

Is a tsunami just one wave?

No. It is a series of waves, and later waves can be larger than the first.

What is the "Tsunami Ready" programme?

A UNESCO-IOC community programme. Villages in Odisha were recognised as Tsunami Ready after building warning systems, evacuation plans, and doing drills.
